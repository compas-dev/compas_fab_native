    Third-party code, data and algorithms¤

    compas_fab_native
Copyright (c) 2026 Gramazio Kohler Research

This repository contains code ported from, data derived from, and algorithms that closely
follow the third-party works listed below. Their licenses are in `licenses/`.


1. ur_kinematics (ROS-Industrial universal_robot)
   https://github.com/ros-industrial/universal_robot/tree/indigo-devel/ur_kinematics
   Copyright (c) 2013, Georgia Institute of Technology. Author: Kelsey Hawkins.
   License: BSD-3-Clause (licenses/ur_kinematics-BSD-3-Clause.txt)

   Used in: crates/motion_core/src/ik.rs (`offset_wrist_ik`, `offset_wrist_fk`): a Rust port
   of the closed-form inverse and forward kinematics of `ur_kin.cpp`, by way of compas_fab's
   `offset_wrist.py` (https://github.com/compas-dev/compas_fab, MIT License, Copyright (c)
   2018-2026 Gramazio Kohler Research).


2. VAMP: Vector-Accelerated Motion Planning
   https://github.com/KavrakiLab/vamp
   Authors: Zachary Kingston, Wil Thomason, Clayton Ramsey and contributors (Kavraki Lab).
   License: Apache-2.0 (licenses/Apache-2.0.txt)
   W. Thomason, Z. Kingston, L. E. Kavraki, "Motions in Microseconds via Vectorized
   Sampling-Based Planning", ICRA 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14545

   Used in:
   - crates/motion_core/src/freespace.rs: RRT-Connect with dynamic domain and tree
     balancing, the Halton sampler bases, and the shortcut and B-spline simplification
     follow VAMP's `planning/rrtc.hh`, `planning/simplify.hh`, `random/halton.hh` and their
     default settings (`rrtc_settings.hh`, `simplify_settings.hh`). Rewritten in Rust;
     modified: f32 node storage per tree, multi-goal handling, no allocations per iteration.
   - crates/motion_core/src/check.rs: the motion check "rake" of `planning/validate.hh`
     (lanes spread over the motion, stepping back together), the sphere/cuboid test of
     `collision/sphere_cuboid.hh`, link bounding spheres before link spheres, and early exit.
     Rewritten in Rust on `wide` SIMD vectors.
   - fixtures/vamp_panda.json, fixtures/vamp_ur5.json: kinematic trees, collision spheres
     and disabled pairs converted from VAMP's `resources/panda/panda_spherized.urdf`,
     `panda.srdf`, `resources/ur5/ur5_spherized.urdf` and `ur5.srdf`
     (scripts/export_vamp_robot.py).
   - crates/motion_bench/src/mbm.rs: VAMP's rules for turning MotionBenchMaker problems into
     obstacles (`problem_dict_to_vamp`). The problems themselves are not included.


3. robowflex_resources (robot descriptions used by VAMP and MotionBenchMaker)
   https://github.com/KavrakiLab/robowflex_resources
   Copyright (c) 2020 Kavraki Lab.
   License: MIT (licenses/robowflex_resources-MIT.txt)
   Z. Kingston, L. E. Kavraki, "Robowflex: Robot motion planning with MoveIt made easy",
   IROS 2022.

   The robot descriptions behind fixtures/vamp_*.json. They derive in turn from:
   - Franka Emika Panda description, franka_ros (https://github.com/frankaemika/franka_ros),
     Apache-2.0.
   - Universal Robots UR5 description, ROS-Industrial universal_robot
     (https://github.com/ros-industrial/universal_robot), BSD-3-Clause and Apache-2.0.
   - Robotiq 2F-85 gripper and FT300 sensor descriptions, ROS-Industrial robotiq
     (https://github.com/ros-industrial/robotiq), BSD-2-Clause.


4. Cephes Mathematical Library
   Stephen L. Moshier, https://www.netlib.org/cephes/
   "Cephes Math Library Release 2.x", distributed freely; see the netlib readme.

   Used in: crates/motion_core/src/check.rs (`sin_cos`): the single-precision polynomial
   coefficients of `sinf` and `cosf` on [-pi/4, pi/4].


5. MotionBenchMaker
   C. Chamzas, C. Quintero-Peña, Z. Kingston, A. Orthey, D. Rakita, M. Gleicher,
   M. Toussaint, L. E. Kavraki, "MotionBenchMaker: A Tool to Generate and Benchmark Motion
   Planning Datasets", IEEE RA-L 2022. https://github.com/KavrakiLab/motion_bench_maker
   Copyright (c) 2021, Rice University. License: BSD-3-Clause.

   The benchmark problems used in SPIKE.md and results/mbm_m3max.md (as shipped by VAMP).
   They are read from a local VAMP checkout and not included here.


6. Algorithms implemented from publications (no code taken)
   - crates/motion_core/src/timing.rs, path with circular blends: T. Kunz, M. Stilman,
     "Time-Optimal Trajectory Generation for Path Following with Bounded Acceleration and
     Velocity", Robotics: Science and Systems 2012.
   - crates/motion_core/src/timing.rs, time parameterization: H. Pham, Q.-C. Pham, "A New
     Approach to Time-Optimal Path Parameterization Based on Reachability Analysis", IEEE
     Transactions on Robotics 34(3), 2018.
   - crates/motion_core/src/freespace.rs: J. J. Kuffner, S. M. LaValle, "RRT-Connect: An
     Efficient Approach to Single-Query Path Planning", ICRA 2000; dynamic domain:
     A. Yershova, L. Jaillet, T. Siméon, S. M. LaValle, "Dynamic-Domain RRTs", ICRA 2005.
   - crates/motion_core/src/ik.rs, spherical wrist: product of exponentials and the
     Paden-Kahan subproblems (R. M. Murray, Z. Li, S. S. Sastry, "A Mathematical
     Introduction to Robotic Manipulation", CRC Press 1994).
   - crates/motion_core/src/numik.rs, numerical IK: Levenberg-Marquardt (K. Levenberg, "A
     Method for the Solution of Certain Non-Linear Problems in Least Squares", Quarterly
     of Applied Mathematics 2(2), 1944; D. W. Marquardt, "An Algorithm for Least-Squares
     Estimation of Nonlinear Parameters", SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 11(2), 1963).
     IK as one weighted least-squares problem over pose, rest-posture and collision costs
     follows PyRoki: C. M. Kim, B. Yi, H. Choi, Y. Ma, K. Goldberg, A. Kanazawa, "PyRoki: A
     Modular Toolkit for Robot Kinematic Optimization", IROS 2025,
     https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03728 (https://github.com/chungmin99/pyroki, MIT License,
     Copyright (c) 2025 Chung Min Kim). The approach only; no PyRoki code is used (PyRoki
     differentiates JAX code automatically, numik.rs writes the Jacobians out).


Rust crates compiled into the Python extension keep their own licenses; their texts are in
`licenses/THIRD_PARTY_RUST.txt` (generated with cargo-about, see `about.toml`), which wheels
ship. Python packages installed as dependencies (`pyproject.toml`) keep theirs.

    The license texts are in the licenses/ folder of the repository, including those of the Rust crates compiled into the extension (THIRD_PARTY_RUST.txt).
